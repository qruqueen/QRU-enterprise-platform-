Story

Maya was asked to use an AI tool to help plan a community event. At first, she treated the tool like a magic answer machine. It made a schedule, wrote messages, and suggested a budget. But Maya noticed something important: some details were wrong, some suggestions did not fit her community, and the tool did not explain where its information came from. So she paused and used AI literacy. She asked: What data might this tool be using? What can it do well? Where might it be uncertain? Could its answer be biased? Is anyone’s private information at risk? Who is responsible for the final decision? Maya still used the AI, but she checked facts, protected personal data, asked people for feedback, and made the final choices herself. That is AI literacy: not fearing AI, not blindly trusting it, but understanding, evaluating, and using it thoughtfully with human judgment.

Call to Action

The next time you use an AI tool, pause before accepting its answer: check one fact, look for one possible bias or limitation, and decide what human judgment is still needed.
