    Slide 2 — Sleep Is Active Maintenance

    SLIDE 4/34
    • Sleep is an essential biological process
    • The brain and body remain active during sleep
    • Sleep supports thinking, mood, immunity, and recovery
    • Rest lowers effort, but sleep performs different work
    • **Plain-language definition:**
    • **Sleep** = a natural state where the brain and body cycle through stages that support health and daily functioning.

    Slide 3 — What Happens During Sleep?

    SLIDE 5/34
    • Humans cycle through two major types of sleep:

    Non-REM Sleep

    SLIDE 6/34
    • Includes lighter sleep and deeper sleep
    • Helps the body slow down and recover
    • Supports physical restoration and immune function

    REM Sleep

    SLIDE 7/34
    • Brain activity increases
    • Dreaming is common
    • Supports learning, memory, and emotional processing
    • **Key idea:**
    • Healthy sleep includes repeated cycles of non-REM and REM sleep.
    • **Footer:** QRU | Understanding Sleep and Rest

    Slide 4 — Why the Brain Needs Sleep

    SLIDE 8/34
    • Improves attention and focus
    • Supports learning and memory
    • Helps decision-making and reaction time
    • Supports emotional regulation
    • Helps the brain clear certain waste products
    • **Plain-language example:**
    • After poor sleep, people may think more slowly, react later, or feel more emotionally reactive.
    • **Footer:** QRU | Understanding Sleep and Rest

    Slide 5 — Why the Body Needs Sleep

    SLIDE 9/34
    • Helps regulate the immune system
    • Supports heart and blood vessel health
    • Helps regulate hormones and appetite
    • Supports growth, repair, and recovery
    • Helps the body maintain energy balance
    • **Plain-language example:**
    • Poor sleep can make the body feel run down and make daily stress harder to handle.
    • **Footer:** QRU | Understanding Sleep and Rest

    Slide 6 — How Much Sleep Do People Need?

    SLIDE 10/34
    • Sleep needs change by:
    • Age
    • Health status
    • Pregnancy
    • Stress level
    • Work schedule
    • Individual biology
    • **Most adults generally need at least 7 hours per night.**

    Slide 7 — Sleep, Rest, Meditation, and Naps Are Not the Same

    SLIDE 11/34
    • | State | What It Can Do | What It Cannot Fully Do |
    • |---|---|---|
    • | **Quiet rest** | Reduces effort and stress | Replace full sleep cycles |
    • | **Meditation** | Supports calm and focus | Provide all sleep functions |
    • | **Nap** | Boosts short-term alertness | Fully replace regular sleep |
    • | **Full sleep** | Provides repeated sleep stages | Cannot be skipped without consequences |
    • **Key idea:**
    • Rest helps, but it is not the same as sleep.

    Slide 8 — Can Rest Replace Sleep?

    SLIDE 12/34
    • Rest can help you feel calmer.
    • Rest can reduce strain.
    • Rest can support recovery after effort.
    • But rest does **not** fully replace sleep.
    • **Why?**
    • Rest does not reliably create the full sleep-stage cycles the brain and body need.
    • **Footer:** QRU | Understanding Sleep and Rest
    • **Speaker Notes:**

    Slide 10 — What Rest Cannot Do

    SLIDE 14/34
    • Rest cannot reliably:
    • Replace deep non-REM sleep
    • Replace REM sleep
    • Restore reaction time after major sleep loss
    • Eliminate drowsy-driving risk
    • Treat sleep disorders
    • Make chronic sleep deprivation safe

    Slide 11 — Practical Sleep Hygiene: Start With Timing

    SLIDE 15/34
    • Wake up at about the same time daily
    • Use weekends carefully
    • Build the bedtime around the wake time
    • Avoid “catching up” in ways that disrupt Monday
    • **Example:**
    • If your target wake time is 6:30 a.m., keep weekends within about 1 hour when possible.

    Slide 12 — Practical Sleep Hygiene: Use Light Wisely

    SLIDE 16/34

      Morning

      SLIDE 17/34

      • Outdoor light is especially helpful

      Evening

      SLIDE 18/34
      • Dim lights before bed
      • Reduce bright screens close to bedtime

      Slide 13 — Practical Sleep Hygiene: Caffeine, Alcohol, and Devices

      SLIDE 19/34

        Caffeine

        SLIDE 20/34
        • Avoid late-day caffeine
        • Many people need a 6–8 hour cutoff

        Alcohol

        SLIDE 21/34
        • May make you sleepy at first
        • Can fragment sleep later
        • May worsen snoring or breathing problems

        Devices

        SLIDE 22/34
        • Set a screen cutoff or “quiet mode”
        • Keep stressful content out of bed

        Slide 16 — Naps: Helpful, but Use Carefully

        SLIDE 25/34
        • Naps may improve:
        • Alertness
        • Mood
        • Reaction time
        • Short-term performance
        • **Use carefully:**
        • Keep many naps short: about 20–30 minutes
        • Avoid late-day naps if they hurt nighttime sleep

        Slide 17 — When to Seek Professional Help

        SLIDE 26/34
        • Seek professional guidance if you have:
        • Insomnia that lasts or keeps returning
        • Loud snoring, choking, or gasping during sleep
        • Excessive daytime sleepiness
        • Trouble functioning because of shift work
        • Morning headaches or dry mouth with snoring
        • Restless legs that disrupt sleep
        • Falling asleep while driving
