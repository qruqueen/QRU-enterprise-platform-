Slide 1 — Sleep Is Not “Doing Nothing”

SLIDE 2/19
  • **On-Slide Content**
  • Sleep is a biologically essential process.
  • It supports the brain, body, emotions, and recovery.
  • Most adults need **7 or more hours** per night.
  • Rest helps, but it does **not** fully replace sleep.
  • **Design Direction:**
  • Use a simple visual of a sleeping person with subtle icons around them: brain, heart, shield, battery.

SLIDE 3/19
  • **On-Slide Content**
  • By the end, learners should be able to:
  • 1. Explain why humans need sleep.
  • 2. Describe what happens during sleep.
  • 3. Distinguish **rest** from **sleep**.
  • 4. Apply practical habits that support healthier sleep.
  • 5. Identify when to seek medical help.
  • **Key Terms**

Slide 3 — How Much Sleep Do People Need?

SLIDE 4/19
  • **On-Slide Content**
  • Most adults need **7 or more hours** per night.
  • Teens and children usually need more than adults.
  • Sleep needs change across life stages.
  • Individual needs vary; “enough” means functioning well.
  • Consistent timing helps support the body’s rhythm.
  • **Design Direction:**
  • Use a simple age-based sleep need graphic. Keep numbers large and easy to read.

Slide 4 — The Two Sleep Drivers

SLIDE 5/19
  • **On-Slide Content**
  • Sleep is shaped mainly by two systems:
  • **Circadian rhythm:** the body’s internal 24-hour clock.
  • **Sleep pressure:** the need for sleep that builds while awake.
  • Light strongly influences the circadian rhythm.
  • Sleep pressure lowers during sleep.
  • **Design Direction:**
  • Show two simple gauges: a 24-hour clock and a “sleep pressure” battery filling during the day.

Slide 5 — Sleep Has Stages

SLIDE 6/19
  • **On-Slide Content**
  • Sleep cycles through stages across the night:
  • **N1:** light transition into sleep.
  • **N2:** stable sleep; body activity slows.
  • **N3:** deep sleep; physical restoration is emphasized.
  • **REM:** active dreaming; learning and emotion processing.
  • **Design Direction:**
  • Use a simple wave graphic showing sleep cycling from N1 to N2 to N3 to REM several times.

Slide 6 — Sleep and Memory

SLIDE 7/19
  • **On-Slide Content**
  • Sleep supports learning by helping the brain:
  • Strengthen important memories.
  • Connect new information with older knowledge.
  • Reduce interference from less useful information.
  • Prepare for attention and learning the next day.
  • **Design Direction:**
  • Show a simple brain icon with “file folders” labeled learn, sort, store, recall.

Slide 7 — Sleep and Emotional Regulation

SLIDE 8/19
  • **On-Slide Content**
  • Sleep helps the brain manage emotions by supporting:
  • Stress recovery.
  • Mood stability.
  • Flexible thinking.
  • Impulse control.
  • Resilience after difficult experiences.

Slide 8 — Sleep and Whole-Body Health

SLIDE 9/19
  • **On-Slide Content**
  • Sleep supports several body systems:
  • **Immune:** defense and inflammation control.
  • **Metabolic:** appetite, glucose, and energy regulation.
  • **Cardiovascular:** blood pressure and heart health.
  • **Recovery:** tissue repair and physical restoration.
  • **Design Direction:**
  • Use four icons: shield, fuel gauge, heart, and repair tool.

Slide 9 — Rest Is Valuable, But Different

SLIDE 10/19
  • **On-Slide Content**
  • Rest can help by:
  • Reducing stress and mental load.
  • Lowering stimulation.
  • Supporting recovery between tasks.
  • Improving attention for a short period.
  • Creating conditions that make sleep easier.
  • **Design Direction:**

Slide 10 — Can Rest Replace Sleep?

SLIDE 11/19
  • **On-Slide Content**
  • **Short answer: No, not fully.**
  • Rest can support recovery, but sleep is biologically distinct.
  • | Option | What It Can Help With | What It Cannot Fully Replace |
  • |---|---|---|
  • | **Sleep** | Brain, body, immune, metabolic, emotional recovery | Not applicable |
  • | **Quiet rest** | Stress relief, mental pause, lower stimulation | Sleep stages and full sleep functions |
  • | **Meditation** | Calm, attention, relaxation | Deep sleep and REM sleep processes |

Slide 11 — Practical Habits That Support Sleep

SLIDE 12/19
  • **On-Slide Content**
  • Keep a consistent wake time when possible.
  • Get bright light earlier in the day.
  • Limit caffeine later in the day.
  • Reduce alcohol close to bedtime.
  • Use a calming wind-down routine.
  • **Design Direction:**
  • Use a checklist layout with small icons: clock, sun, coffee cup, wine glass, moon.

Slide 12 — Sleep Environment, Screens, and Naps

SLIDE 13/19
  • **On-Slide Content**
  • Keep the bedroom cool, dark, quiet, and comfortable.
  • Reduce bright screens and light before bed.
  • Use the bed mainly for sleep when possible.
  • Nap early and briefly if naps affect nighttime sleep.
  • Avoid drowsy driving; sleepiness is a safety risk.
  • **Design Direction:**
  • Use a bedroom icon with adjustable sliders labeled cool, dark, quiet, comfortable.

Slide 13 — When to Seek Medical Help

SLIDE 14/19
  • **On-Slide Content**
  • Consider professional help if you experience:
  • Insomnia or poor sleep lasting several weeks.
  • Loud snoring, gasping, or witnessed pauses in breathing.
  • Severe daytime sleepiness or drowsy driving risk.
  • Restless, uncomfortable legs that disrupt sleep.
  • Shift-work sleep problems or safety concerns.
