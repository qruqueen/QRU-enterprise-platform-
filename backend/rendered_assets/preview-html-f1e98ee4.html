Introduction
  • Love is a complex human experience involving attachment, caregiving, sexual desire, emotion regulation, and social bonding, shaped by both biological bonding systems and cultural relationship practices.
Why It Matters

Understanding love helps people see that healthy relationships are not built only on intense feelings, but also on care, trust, support, and repeated choices; supportive loving relationships are linked to better mental health, lower stress, and improved physical health outcomes, while isolation and relationship distress are linked to increased health risks.

How It Works

Love can begin with attraction or romantic intensity, but it also involves attachment, caregiving, emotional support, and social bonding over time. Research in psychology and neuroscience associates romantic love and attachment with reward, motivation, oxytocin, vasopressin, dopamine, and long-term pair-bonding systems. Culture strongly shapes how love is expressed, valued, and practiced.

The Core Idea
  • Love is like a garden: an initial bloom may appear through attraction, but lasting love depends on ongoing care, protection, attention, and the surrounding environment.
