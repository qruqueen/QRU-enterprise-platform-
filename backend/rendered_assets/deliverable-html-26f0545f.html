Step by Step

  • Start with a sport the child already enjoys so the learning feels emotionally connected.
  • Introduce multiplication as “many groups combined,” not just a fact to memorize.
  • Create a sports example, such as a quarterback completing 5 passes each quarter for 4 quarters.
  • Turn the sports situation into a multiplication sentence: 5 × 4 = 20.
  • Ask the child to explain the strategy they used, because explaining builds understanding.
  • Practice with new examples using repetition, feedback, and adjustment.
  • Treat mistakes as training reps and ask, “What can we learn from this?”
  • Reflect like watching game film: What improved? What still needs practice? What strategy helped?
  • Apply the same thinking to another sport or real-life situation.
  • Celebrate growth, effort, reasoning, and confidence—not just the final answer.
