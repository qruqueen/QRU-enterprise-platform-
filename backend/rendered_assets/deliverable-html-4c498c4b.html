Every learner has the possibility to grow stronger in understanding. QRU Sports Intelligence Lab™ helps transform math learning into an athlete-training experience by connecting mathematics to sports, strategy, competition, practice, teamwork, decision-making, and reflection.

The learner becomes a Brain Athlete.

  • Confidence
  • Curiosity
  • Persistence
  • Reasoning
  • Communication
  • Self-awareness
  • Problem solving
  • Lifelong learning ability

Key Vocabulary

Multiplication

Intelligence

Lesson Objectives

By the end of this lesson, learners will be able to:

1. Explain multiplication as many groups combined.

2. Connect sports practice to repeated thinking practice.

3. Solve simple sports-based multiplication problems.

4. Explain their strategy instead of only giving an answer.

5. Reflect on how practice, mistakes, and adjustment support learning.

6. Recognize that struggling with math does not mean someone is “bad at math.”

Lesson Frame: The QRU Path

This lesson follows the QRU Translation™ learning sequence:

Question → Understanding → Practice → Connection → Application → Reflection → Growth

9

1. Opening: The Brain Athlete Invitation

Begin with this idea:

Every athlete starts as a rookie. They enter practice, train, struggle, improve, and learn that champions are not created by talent alone. Champions are created through intentional practice.

Now connect that to learning:

A Brain Athlete trains the mind. The brain becomes stronger through repeated meaningful practice. Mistakes are not proof of failure. In this lab, mistakes are training reps.

Teach the Analogy

  • Brain = Muscle
  • Practice = Training
  • Mistakes = Training Reps
  • Knowledge = Equipment
  • Understanding = Game Strategy
  • Reflection = Game Film Review
10

2. The Question

Ask:

Beginner Question

What is multiplication?

Guide learners toward the QRU definition:

Multiplication means many groups combined.

Example:

If a player completes the same number of actions again and again, multiplication helps us find the total.

11

3. Understanding: Sports Make the Math Visible

Explain:

Math is not only something on a worksheet. Sports are full of patterns, groups, repeated actions, and decisions.

When athletes track performance, they often need to understand repeated groups.

Football Example

A quarterback completes 5 passes each quarter.

There are 4 quarters.

So:

5 × 4 = 20 passes

Meaning:

There are 4 groups of 5 passes.

Basketball Example

A player makes 8 free throws each practice.

The player practices 5 times.

So:

8 × 5 = 40 free throws

Meaning:

There are 5 groups of 8 free throws.

The math becomes connected to athletic progress.

12

4. Practice: Train the Thinking

Invite learners to solve and explain.

Practice Problem 1: Football

A quarterback completes 5 passes each quarter for 4 quarters.

Ask:

1. What are the groups?

2. How many are in each group?

3. What multiplication sentence shows the total?

4. What is the answer?

Expected solution:

5 × 4 = 20 passes

Practice Problem 2: Basketball

A basketball player makes 8 free throws each practice for 5 practices.

Ask:

1. What is repeated?

2. How many practices are there?

3. What does the multiplication sentence mean?

4. What is the total?

Expected solution:

8 × 5 = 40 free throws

13

5. Connection: Math Is Strategy, Not Just Speed

Teach clearly:

Multiplication is not:

  • Memorizing without understanding
  • Racing against others
  • Measuring intelligence

Multiplication is a way to understand repeated groups and patterns.

A learner who needs more practice is not “bad at math.” They may need:

  • More practice
  • Different explanations
  • A different strategy

This preserves confidence and keeps the door open for growth.

14

6. Application Activity: Design Your Own Sports Problem

Learners create their own sports-based multiplication problem.

They should include:

1. A sport or athletic activity

2. A repeated action

3. The number in each group

4. The number of groups

5. A multiplication sentence

6. The total

7. A short explanation of their strategy

Example Structure

A player does the same action several times across practices, quarters, rounds, or games.

Learners explain:

  • “My groups are…”
  • “Each group has…”
  • “My multiplication sentence is…”
  • “The total is…”
  • “My strategy was…”
15

7. Reflection: Game Film Review for the Brain

Reflection is like watching game film after practice. You review what happened, notice patterns, adjust your strategy, and come back stronger next time.

Ask learners:

1. What did you understand better after practice?

2. What felt difficult?

3. What strategy helped you?

4. What would you try differently next time?

5. How can mistakes become training reps?

The adult’s role is to be a:

  • Coach
  • Facilitator
  • Encourager
  • Guide

The goal is not perfection.

The goal is helping the learner discover:

> “I can learn difficult things.”

Use language that builds capability:

  • “Let’s look for the pattern.”
  • “What are the groups?”
  • “What is being repeated?”
  • “Explain your strategy.”
  • “That mistake gives us information.”
  • “Let’s adjust and try again.”
