    Activity 3: Strengths and Limits

    • Choose one AI tool or AI use case.
    • What can it help with?
    • What can it not reliably do?

    Activity 5

    Activity 4: AI Output Evaluation Checklist

    • Use this checklist to review an AI-generated output.
    • Accuracy: What needs to be checked?
    • Source quality: What evidence would strengthen the output?
    • Bias: Whose perspective might be missing?
    • Privacy: Was any sensitive information used or requested?
    • Security: Could this create a risk if misused?
    • Transparency: Is it clear how or why the output was produced?
    • Accountability: Who is responsible for using the result?

    • Scenario: A school, workplace, clinic, or public agency wants to use an AI system to support decisions.

    • 3. What questions should people ask before adopting it?
    • 4. Who should be involved in the decision?

    Activity 8

    Practice Questions

    • In your own words, define AI literacy without using the phrase “artificial intelligence.”
    • List three places where AI may appear in everyday life, such as education, work, healthcare, media, or public services.
    • Choose one AI tool or system you have used or seen. What does it seem to do well, and what should a human still check?
    • Explain the difference between an automated output and human judgment.
    • Identify two possible risks of using AI-generated information without checking it.
    • Create a short checklist someone could use before trusting an AI-generated answer.
    • Describe why privacy matters when entering personal information into an AI system.
    • Give one example of how bias could affect an AI system’s output or decision.
    Activity 9

    Reflection Questions

    • Where in your life do you already interact with AI, even if you do not always notice it?
    • How confident do you feel in judging whether an AI output is accurate or trustworthy? What would help you improve?
    • When should a person rely on AI assistance, and when should they slow down and use stronger human oversight?
    • What kinds of personal information would you avoid sharing with an AI system, and why?
    • How might AI literacy help people participate more fairly in decisions that affect education, work, healthcare, media, or public services?
    • What responsibility do users have when sharing AI-generated content with others?
    • How can learning about AI’s limitations make someone more powerful rather than more fearful?
    • What does responsible AI use look like in your community, school, workplace, or family?
