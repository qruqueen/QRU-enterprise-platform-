Short Video Production Script

QRU Factory Acceptance Test — Understanding Sleep and Rest

Topic: Why do humans need sleep, and can rest replace it?

Length: ~90 seconds

Format: Educational short video with narration, visuals, and on-screen text

Tone: Warm, clear, trustworthy, practical

Scene 1 — Opening Hook: “Is Rest Enough?”

Time: 0:00–0:08

Visuals:

A tired adult sits at a desk late at night, rubbing their eyes. Cut to the same person lying on a couch during the day with eyes closed, trying to “rest.”

Narration:

“Have you ever wondered: if I rest during the day, can that replace a bad night of sleep?”

On-Screen Text:

Can rest replace sleep?

Scene 2 — The Core Answer

Time: 0:08–0:18

Visuals:

Split screen: one side shows a person calmly resting in a chair; the other shows a person asleep in a dark, quiet bedroom. Gentle animation highlights the brain, heart, immune system, and muscles during sleep.

Narration:

“Rest helps reduce physical or mental strain. But sleep is different. Sleep is a biologically essential process that supports the brain, body, and emotions.”

On-Screen Text:

Rest reduces strain.

Sleep restores systems.

Scene 3 — What Sleep Supports

Time: 0:18–0:34

Visuals:

Clean icon sequence: brain, shield/immune system, heart, metabolism symbol, calm face, recovering muscles.

Narration:

“During sufficient, high-quality sleep, your body supports brain function, immune regulation, cardiovascular health, metabolism, emotional regulation, and physical recovery.”

On-Screen Text:

Sleep supports:

  • Brain function
  • Immune regulation
  • Heart health
  • Metabolism
  • Emotional balance
  • Physical recovery

Scene 4 — The Car Analogy

Time: 0:34–0:48

Visuals:

A car pulls into a parking spot and cools down. Then transition to the car entering a service garage where technicians run checks and repairs.

Narration:

“Think of it this way: rest is like parking a car so the engine can cool down. Sleep is like taking the car into the shop for essential maintenance, system checks, and repairs.”

On-Screen Text:

Rest = pause the drain

Sleep = do the repair

Scene 5 — How Much Sleep Do We Need?

Time: 0:48–0:58

Visuals:

Simple timeline graphic: adult icon with “at least 7 hours,” child and teen icons with “more.”

Narration:

“Most adults need at least seven hours of sleep per night. Children and adolescents need more because they are still growing and developing.”

On-Screen Text:

Adults: at least 7 hours/night

Children & teens: more

Scene 6 — What Happens When Sleep Is Too Short

Time: 0:58–1:13

Visuals:

A person after several late nights: irritable at breakfast, choosing high-calorie snacks, losing focus at work, reacting slowly while driving.

Narration:

“When sleep is repeatedly too short, people may feel more irritable, struggle to focus, crave more high-calorie foods, and react more slowly. Over time, chronic insufficient sleep is associated with higher risks of obesity, diabetes, high blood pressure, cardiovascular disease, depression, accidents, and impaired thinking.”

On-Screen Text:

Chronic insufficient sleep is linked with:

Poor focus • Accidents • Depression • Obesity • Diabetes • Hypertension • Cardiovascular disease

Scene 7 — Sleep Hygiene: What Helps

Time: 1:13–1:30

Visuals:

Evening routine montage: setting a regular alarm, putting phone away, dimming lights, avoiding late coffee/alcohol, closing curtains, quiet bedroom.

Narration:

“To support better sleep, keep a consistent sleep and wake schedule, limit caffeine and alcohol near bedtime, reduce evening light and screen exposure, and create a dark, quiet sleep environment.”

On-Screen Text:

Better sleep habits:

  • Consistent schedule
  • Limit caffeine/alcohol near bedtime
  • Reduce evening light/screens
  • Dark, quiet room

Scene 8 — When to Seek Help

Time: 1:30–1:42

Visuals:

A person talking with a healthcare professional. Soft, reassuring tone. Icons for insomnia, daytime sleepiness, snoring, sleep apnea concern.

Narration:

“And if you have persistent insomnia, excessive daytime sleepiness, loud snoring, or suspected sleep apnea, seek clinical care.”

On-Screen Text:

Seek care for:

Persistent insomnia • Excessive daytime sleepiness • Loud snoring • Possible sleep apnea

Scene 9 — Final Memory Line

Time: 1:42–1:50

Visuals:

Calm bedroom scene. Person turns off lamp, room becomes dark and peaceful. QRU-style closing card.

Narration:

“Remember: rest can pause the drain, but sleep does the repair.”

On-Screen Text:

Rest can pause the drain.

Sleep does the repair.

Understanding Sleep and Rest
