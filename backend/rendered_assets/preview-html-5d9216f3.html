Step 2

The Question™

How does my body keep blood glucose steady after I eat carbohydrates?

Try this first:

Imagine you eat a meal with rice, fruit, bread, pasta, potatoes, or sweets. What do you think happens next?

A. The food instantly turns into body fat

B. Carbohydrates are broken down into glucose, which enters the blood

C. Insulin adds sugar to the blood

D. Blood glucose stays exactly the same

Check yourself: The best answer is B. Many carbohydrates are digested into glucose, a simple sugar your body can use for energy. “Blood sugar” usually means blood glucose.

Step 3

Simple Answer™

After you eat carbohydrates, glucose enters your bloodstream. Your pancreas senses the rise and releases insulin. Insulin helps many body cells take in glucose and also signals the liver and muscles to store extra glucose as glycogen for later.

Insulin does not “unlock” every cell in the same way, and some tissues can use glucose without insulin. But insulin is one of the main signals that helps bring blood glucose back toward a healthy range after a meal.
