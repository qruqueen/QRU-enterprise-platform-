Step 1

An Interactive Lesson on Risk, Health, Stewardship, and Accountability

> Core Question: Can faith make day trading healthier or more successful?

> Plain-Language Answer: Faith may help a person practice patience, honesty, self-control, community accountability, and wise stewardship. But faith does not make a trade safer, predict the market, or validate a financial decision. Day trading remains highly risky, and research shows that most individual day traders lose money after costs.

Step 2

Important Disclaimer

This lesson is for education, reflection, and risk awareness only.

It does not recommend day trading. It does not tell you what to buy, sell, hold, or avoid. It does not provide personalized financial, legal, tax, medical, mental health, addiction recovery, or spiritual advice.

If you are making financial decisions, consult qualified professionals such as a licensed financial adviser, tax professional, attorney, mental health clinician, addiction specialist, or trusted faith/community leader as appropriate.

If trading is causing serious distress, secrecy, debt, conflict, sleep problems, panic, suicidal thoughts, or inability to stop, treat that as a serious warning sign and seek help immediately.

Step 3

Emergency and Help Pathway

If you are in immediate danger or may harm yourself:

1. Stop trading now.

2. Move away from screens and trading apps.

3. Contact emergency help immediately.

  • In the U.S. and Canada, call or text 988 for the Suicide & Crisis Lifeline.
  • If there is immediate danger, call your local emergency number, such as 911 in the U.S. and Canada.
  • Outside the U.S. and Canada, contact your local emergency services or a crisis hotline in your country.

4. Tell one safe person: “I am not safe right now and I need help staying away from trading and harming myself.”

If trading feels addictive, compulsive, secretive, or impossible to stop:

  • Consider contacting a licensed mental health professional or addiction specialist.
  • In the U.S., you may contact the National Problem Gambling Helpline: 1-800-GAMBLER or visit ncpgambling.org. Even when trading is not legally gambling, the behavior can become gambling-like for some people.
  • Consider asking your brokerage about account restrictions, cooling-off periods, disabling margin/options access, or closing accounts.
  • Consider speaking with a nonprofit credit counselor, bankruptcy attorney, or qualified financial professional if debt is involved.

> QRU Safety Sentence:

> If trading threatens your safety, relationships, housing, food, sleep, or will to live, the next right trade is no trade. Get help now.

Day trading means buying and selling financial assets quickly, often within the same day, in an attempt to profit from short-term price changes.

A person might day trade:

  • Stocks
  • Options
  • Futures
  • Foreign currencies
  • Cryptocurrency
  • Leveraged exchange-traded funds
  • Other speculative assets

Day trading can feel like trying to make quick decisions in a fast-moving storm.

Prices move quickly. Emotions rise quickly. Mistakes can happen quickly.

A trader may feel:

  • Excited after wins
  • Anxious after losses
  • Pressured to keep watching
  • Tempted to risk more to recover money
  • Embarrassed to tell others what is happening
  • Spiritually confused if they prayed and still lost money

The danger is not only that money can be lost. The danger is that the activity can begin to reshape a person’s habits, emotions, relationships, and sense of peace.

Step 7

Key Vocabulary

| Term | Plain-Language Meaning |

|---|---|

| Day trading | Frequent buying and selling of financial assets, often within the same day, to try to profit from short-term price movement. |

| Speculation | Taking financial risk based on uncertain future price movement. |

| Investing | Buying assets with a longer-term plan based on goals, diversification, time horizon, and risk tolerance. |

| Behavioral addiction | A pattern of behavior that feels hard to control and continues despite harm or distress. |

| Loss-chasing | Risking more money to try to recover previous losses. |

| Margin | Borrowed money from a broker used to buy or trade financial assets. |

| Leverage | Using borrowed money or financial instruments to control a larger position than your cash alone would allow. |

| Liquidity | How easily an asset can be bought or sold without greatly affecting its price. |

| Volatility | How much and how quickly prices move up and down. |

| Spread | The difference between the price buyers are willing to pay and sellers are asking. It is a hidden trading cost. |

Step 11

4.1 Price Movement Is Uncertain

Short-term prices can move because of:

  • News
  • Rumors
  • Earnings reports
  • Interest rate changes
  • Large institutional trades
  • Economic data
  • Social media attention
  • Market-maker activity
  • Geopolitical events
  • Sudden fear or excitement

A retail trader usually does not have the same speed, tools, research access, or execution power as professional firms.

Even if a person is intelligent, disciplined, and sincere, the market can still move against them.

Step 12

4.2 Costs Can Quietly Reduce Returns

Even when an app says “commission-free,” trading may still have costs.

Common costs include:

| Cost | What It Means |

|---|---|

| Commissions | Direct fees charged for trades. Many stock trades may be commission-free, but not all products are. Options, futures, and international trades may have fees. |

| Spreads | The gap between buying and selling prices. Frequent trading means repeatedly crossing that gap. |

| Payment for order flow and execution quality | Some brokers route orders in ways that may affect execution quality. |

| Slippage | The price you expect may not be the price you receive, especially in fast or illiquid markets. |

| Platform/data fees | Some tools, data feeds, or premium services cost money. |

| Borrowing costs | Margin loans and short selling can involve interest or fees. |

Small costs can become large when repeated many times.

> QRU Recap Box:

> A trader does not only need to be “right.” A trader must be right enough to overcome spreads, slippage, fees, taxes, and mistakes.

Step 13

4.3 Taxes Can Be Complicated

Frequent trading can create tax consequences.

Depending on your country and situation, tax issues may include:

  • Short-term capital gains taxed differently than long-term gains
  • Loss deduction limits
  • Wash sale rules
  • Complex reporting
  • Tax treatment of options, futures, crypto, or foreign assets
  • Estimated tax payments
  • Recordkeeping burdens

In the United States, short-term capital gains are generally taxed as ordinary income, while long-term capital gains may receive different treatment. Wash sale rules can disallow or defer certain losses when substantially identical securities are repurchased within a specified period.

Tax laws vary by location and change over time. Consult a qualified tax professional.

Step 14

4.4 Margin and Leverage Can Magnify Losses

Margin means borrowing money from a broker. Leverage means controlling a larger position than your cash alone would allow.

Leverage can magnify gains, but it can also magnify losses.

A leveraged trade can lose more quickly than an unleveraged trade. In some products, a person can lose more than the original amount invested. Margin calls can force selling at bad times.

Examples of leveraged or high-risk products include:

  • Options
  • Futures
  • Forex
  • Margin stock trading
  • Leveraged ETFs
  • Certain cryptocurrency derivatives

> Memory Sentence:

> Leverage does not make you smarter; it makes the outcome bigger.

Step 15

4.5 Liquidity Risk Can Trap You

Liquidity means how easily something can be bought or sold.

If an asset is illiquid:

  • You may not be able to sell quickly.
  • You may have to accept a worse price.
  • The spread may be wide.
  • Price movement may be extreme.

This can be especially important in:

  • Thinly traded stocks
  • Some options contracts
  • Small cryptocurrencies
  • After-hours markets
  • Panic conditions
Step 16

4.6 Volatility Can Overwhelm Judgment

Volatility means prices move up and down sharply.

Volatility can create opportunity, but it also creates danger:

  • Stop-loss orders may trigger quickly.
  • Prices may gap past your stop.
  • Emotional decisions become more likely.
  • A trader may increase size to recover losses.
  • A winning trade can turn into a losing trade suddenly.

Volatility is not proof of opportunity. It is proof of movement.

Step 17

4.7 Pattern Day Trader Rules May Apply

In the United States, FINRA pattern day trader rules may apply to certain margin accounts.

A person may be considered a pattern day trader if they execute four or more day trades within five business days in a margin account, and those trades represent more than 6% of total trades in that period. Pattern day traders are generally required to maintain at least $25,000 in equity in the margin account before day trading.

Rules may vary by account type, product, broker, and jurisdiction. Always verify current rules with the broker and applicable regulator.

Important: Having enough money to meet a rule does not mean day trading is wise or safe.

Research on individual active trading and day trading is sobering.

Multiple studies have found that frequent individual traders tend to underperform, and that most day traders do not earn sustained profits after costs.

A widely cited study by Barber and Odean found that individual investors who traded more frequently earned lower net returns, and that trading was “hazardous” to wealth. Research by Barber, Lee, Liu, and Odean on Taiwanese day traders found that most day traders lost money after transaction costs, while only a small minority showed persistent profitability. A study of Brazilian day traders by Chague, De-Losso, and Giovannetti found that the vast majority lost money and that only a very small percentage earned more than a modest daily wage over time.

The lesson is not that profit is impossible. The lesson is that sustained profitability is uncommon, difficult, and not guaranteed by intelligence, confidence, effort, or faith.

Step 26

Red Flag Rule

If any of the following are true, pause trading and seek help:

  • You are hiding losses.
  • You are using debt to trade.
  • You are trading money needed for basic needs.
  • You cannot follow your own stop rules.
  • You feel unable to stop.
  • You feel panic, despair, or suicidal thoughts.
  • Your relationships are being harmed.
  • You are lying to keep trading.
  • You are sleeping poorly because of markets.
  • You are using prayer or spiritual certainty to override risk limits.

> Memory Sentence:

> Secrecy is a stop sign.
