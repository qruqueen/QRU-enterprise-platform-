🌙 Sleep Is Not “Doing Nothing”

Sleep is an essential body process that helps your brain and body recover, reset, and prepare for the next day.

Quality sleep supports:

  • Memory and learning
  • Mood and emotional regulation
  • Focus, decision-making, and reaction time
  • Immune system function
  • Growth and development in children and teens
  • Heart, metabolism, and overall health
  • Safety at school, work, home, and on the road

> Key reminder: Rest is not a reward. Rest is part of healthy functioning.

---

⏰ How Much Sleep Do We Need?

Sleep needs vary by age, health, activity level, schedule, and individual medical needs. These are general daily ranges, including naps for younger children. Some people may need more or less sleep based on medical guidance.

| Age Group | General Recommended Sleep |

|---|---:|

| Newborns, 0–3 months | 14–17 hours |

| Infants, 4–12 months | 12–16 hours |

| Toddlers, 1–2 years | 11–14 hours |

| Preschoolers, 3–5 years | 10–13 hours |

| School-age children, 6–12 years | 9–12 hours |

| Teens, 13–18 years | 8–10 hours |

| Adults, 18–64 years | 7 or more hours |

| Older adults, 65+ years | 7–8 hours |

🚩 Signs You May Not Be Getting Enough Rest

You may need more sleep or better-quality sleep if you regularly experience:

  • Trouble waking up or needing repeated alarms
  • Daytime sleepiness or dozing off unintentionally
  • Difficulty concentrating, remembering, or learning
  • Irritability, low mood, stress, or emotional outbursts
  • Headaches, low energy, or feeling “foggy”
  • More mistakes, slower reaction time, or clumsiness
  • Relying heavily on caffeine to get through the day
  • Falling asleep during class, work, conversations, or driving

🧠 The QRU REST Routine

Use REST to remember four core habits that support better sleep.

R — Regular Schedule

Go to bed and wake up around the same time each day, including weekends when possible.

E — Environment Matters

Keep your sleep space dark, quiet, cool, and comfortable. Use curtains, earplugs, white noise, or an eye mask if helpful.

S — Screens Down

Reduce screen use before bedtime. Bright light and stimulating content can make it harder to fall asleep.

Try this:

  • Put devices away 30–60 minutes before bed when possible.
  • Use night mode or dim settings if screens are necessary.
  • Keep phones away from the bed to reduce scrolling and alerts.

T — Time to Wind Down

Create a calm transition from daytime activity to sleep.

Helpful wind-down habits include:

  • Reading something relaxing
  • Gentle stretching
  • Prayer, meditation, or breathing exercises
  • Journaling tomorrow’s to-do list
  • Quiet music or a calming routine
  • Lowering lights and reducing noise

> Bonus T — Talk to a Professional: If sleep problems continue or affect daily life, speak with a healthcare professional.

✅ Sleep Hygiene Checklist

Try these practical steps tonight:

  • ☐ Keep a consistent sleep and wake time.
  • ☐ Create a calming bedtime routine, such as reading, stretching, prayer, journaling, or quiet music.
  • ☐ Turn down lights in the evening.
  • ☐ Put phones, tablets, computers, and TVs away before bed.
  • ☐ Avoid caffeine in the late afternoon and evening.
  • ☐ Avoid heavy meals close to bedtime if they disturb your sleep.
  • ☐ Make the room dark, quiet, cool, and comfortable.
  • ☐ Use the bed mainly for sleep, not studying, scrolling, or working.
  • ☐ Get daylight exposure during the day when possible.
  • ☐ Move your body regularly, but avoid intense exercise right before bedtime if it keeps you awake.

☀️ Rest Is More Than Sleep

Rest can also include short recovery practices during the day:

  • Taking quiet breaks
  • Breathing slowly for 1–2 minutes
  • Stretching
  • Drinking water
  • Spending time outdoors
  • Pausing from screens
  • Asking for help when overwhelmed

Small moments of rest can support energy, focus, and emotional balance.

---

🩺 When to Seek Help

Talk with a healthcare professional if you or someone in your care has:

  • Ongoing insomnia or trouble sleeping for several weeks
  • Loud snoring, choking, gasping, or pauses in breathing during sleep
  • Excessive daytime sleepiness, even after enough time in bed
  • Falling asleep during class, work, conversations, meals, or other daily activities
  • Drowsiness while driving or near-misses related to sleepiness
  • Restless, uncomfortable, or crawling feelings in the legs that interfere with sleep
  • Sleep problems connected to shift work, rotating schedules, or night work
  • Frequent nightmares or trauma-related sleep concerns
  • Sleepwalking, unusual movements, or behaviors during sleep that create safety concerns
  • Sleep problems that affect mood, school, work, relationships, parenting, caregiving, or daily life

> Safety first: Do not drive or operate machinery when sleepy. If you become drowsy while driving, pull over safely, rest, and do not continue until you are alert.

💤 Quick Sleep Reset

When sleep feels off, start with these three steps:

1. Pick a consistent wake-up time.

2. Dim lights and reduce screens before bed.

3. Create a simple wind-down routine you can repeat nightly.

Small, steady changes often work better than trying to fix everything at once.
