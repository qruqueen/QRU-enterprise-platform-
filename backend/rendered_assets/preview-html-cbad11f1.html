Post 1:

**Hook:** Trading is like dating: chemistry is not enough.

A trade can look exciting, but excitement does not make it a good fit.

Before you enter, check whether it matches your plan.

Ask yourself:

- What setup am I looking for?

- Where is my entry?

- Where is my exit if I am wrong?

- How much am I willing to risk?

- Does this trade fit my rules, or am I chasing?

**Dating comparison:** Attraction gets your attention. Standards help you make better decisions.

**Visual idea:** Split-screen carousel: “Excitement” vs. “Standards.” Show a flashy chart on one side and a written checklist on the other.

Post 2:

**Hook:** A healthy trader has boundaries.

In dating, boundaries protect your time, energy, and future.

In trading, boundaries protect your capital and decision-making.

Trading boundaries can include:

- Position sizing: how much you risk on one trade

- Stop-loss rules: where you exit if the trade goes against you

- Profit-taking rules: where you may lock in gains

- Time limits: when you stop trading for the day

- Setup rules: what conditions must be present before you enter

A written trading plan is how your calm self protects you when emotions get loud.

**Visual idea:** Carousel titled “Trader Boundaries.” Each slide shows one boundary with a simple icon: size, exit, profit, time, setup.

Post 3:

**Hook:** The goal is not to prove you are right.

Overconfidence can hurt traders. So can revenge trading, emotional decisions, and taking too many trades just to feel active.

Trading involves risk. Losses are normal, capital can be lost, and no trade is guaranteed.

A stronger goal is:

- Protect your downside

- Follow your plan

- Learn from your decisions

- Avoid risking money you cannot afford to lose

- Stay consistent instead of impulsive

**Dating comparison:** You do not need every person to like you. A trader does not need every trade to win. You need standards, patience, and self-control.

**Visual idea:** Text overlay on a calm background: “You do not need to be right every time. You need to manage risk every time.”

---

Post 4:

**Hook:** Before entering a trade, ask dating-style questions.

Not every opportunity deserves your attention. Slow down before you commit.

Ask:

- Is this actually a good fit for my strategy?

- What are the red flags?

- Am I entering because of a signal or because of fear of missing out?

- Where do I walk away if I am wrong?

- What am I risking?

- What would make this trade no longer valid?

If you cannot answer these clearly, you may not have a trade — you may just have excitement.

**Visual idea:** Checklist carousel: “Before You Enter.” Use green checks for plan-based answers and red flags for emotional reasons.

---

Post 5:

**Hook:** Review the trade, not your worth.

A losing trade does not automatically mean you failed.

A winning trade does not automatically mean you made a good decision.

After each trade, review without shame:

- Did I follow my plan?

- Was my entry valid?

- Was my position size appropriate?

- Did I respect my exit?

- What emotion showed up?

- What should I repeat or improve next time?

Experience becomes wisdom when you study it honestly.

**CTA / Reflection prompt:** Before your next trade, write one sentence: “I will only enter if ______, and I will exit if ______.”

**Visual idea:** Journal-style graphic with two columns: “What happened?” and “What I learned.”
