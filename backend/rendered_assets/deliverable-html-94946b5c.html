Adult Version

QRU Sports Intelligence Lab uses sports as an emotional and practical doorway into mathematics. The child becomes a “Brain Athlete,” learning that multiplication is not simply memorization, but the combining of equal groups. A sports example makes the concept concrete: 5 completed passes per quarter across 4 quarters becomes 5 × 4 = 20 passes. This approach connects math to practice, strategy, feedback, confidence, and reflection. The caregiver or teacher acts like a coach: not demanding perfection, but helping the learner discover, “I can learn difficult things.” The deeper goal is not only mathematical accuracy. It is developing reasoning, persistence, communication, decision-making, and self-awareness.

Step by Step

  • Start with a sport the child already enjoys so the learning feels emotionally connected.
  • Introduce multiplication as “many groups combined,” not just a fact to memorize.
  • Create a sports example, such as a quarterback completing 5 passes each quarter for 4 quarters.
  • Turn the sports situation into a multiplication sentence: 5 × 4 = 20.
  • Ask the child to explain the strategy they used, because explaining builds understanding.
  • Practice with new examples using repetition, feedback, and adjustment.
  • Treat mistakes as training reps and ask, “What can we learn from this?”
  • Reflect like watching game film: What improved? What still needs practice? What strategy helped?
  • Apply the same thinking to another sport or real-life situation.
  • Celebrate growth, effort, reasoning, and confidence—not just the final answer.

Lesson Plan

Lesson Plan: Sports Multiplication for Brain Athletes

Title

QRU Sports Intelligence Lab™: Multiplication as Sports Training

Age Group

Children learning beginner multiplication

Core Message

Multiplication means many groups combined. Brain Athletes improve through practice, feedback, adjustment, and reflection.

Learning Goals

Students will be able to:

  • Explain multiplication as many groups combined.
  • Solve multiplication problems using sports examples.
  • Create their own sports multiplication problem.
  • Reflect on one strategy that helped them learn.

Materials

  • Whiteboard or paper
  • Sports images or objects such as balls, cones, or scorecards
  • Pencils
  • Student workbook page

Key Vocabulary

  • Multiplication: many groups combined
  • Strategy: a plan for solving a problem
  • Reflection: thinking about what helped and what to improve
  • Brain Athlete: a learner who trains the mind through practice

Lesson Flow

1. Warm-Up: Athlete Thinking

Ask students:

  • How do athletes get better?
  • Do athletes make mistakes during practice?
  • What do they do after a mistake?

Teacher connection:

“Learning works in a similar way. Today, we are going to train our brains.”

2. Teach the Concept

Write:

Multiplication = many groups combined

Example:

5 passes each quarter × 4 quarters = 20 passes

Ask:

  • What is one group?
  • How many groups are there?
  • What is the total?

3. Guided Practice

Problem 1:

A basketball player makes 8 free throws each practice. The player practices 5 times. How many free throws?

Answer: 8 × 5 = 40

Problem 2:

A soccer player scores 3 goals each week for 4 weeks. How many goals?

Answer: 3 × 4 = 12

4. Strategy Talk

Students explain how they solved one problem.

Possible strategies:

  • Skip counting
  • Drawing groups
  • Repeated addition
  • Using a known fact

5. Create Your Own Sports Problem

Students choose a sport and create a multiplication story.

Example frame:

A ______ does ______ each ______ for ______ groups. How many total?

6. Reflection

Students complete:

  • One thing I understand better now is...
  • One strategy I used was...
  • One mistake that helped me learn was...

Teacher Notes

The teacher is a coach, facilitator, encourager, and guide. Avoid speed pressure. Focus on understanding, explanation, and confidence.

Closing Memory Sentence

“Champions train their bodies. Brain Athletes train their minds.”

Call to Action

Create your own sports multiplication challenge using the number 36, solve it with a strategy, and explain your thinking like a coach reviewing game film.
