Activity 1

Introduction

  • The brain works by using networks of specialized cells, mainly neurons and glial cells, to process information, coordinate body functions, generate behavior, and support conscious experience.
Activity 2

Why It Matters

  • Understanding the brain as a changing network helps explain why learning, habits, emotions, decisions, and recovery from injury are connected to physical changes in the nervous system.
Activity 3

How It Works

    Neurons communicate using electrical impulses and chemical signals at synapses. Glial cells are specialized brain cells that are part of the brain’s working networks. Different brain regions are specialized for certain functions, but complex abilities usually come from distributed activity across many interconnected areas. Large-scale brain networks coordinate perception, movement, memory, emotion, and decision-making. Because the brain is plastic, its connections can change with learning, experience, development, and injury.

Activity 4

The Core Idea

  • The brain is a changing city of connected systems: specialized areas do different jobs, communication routes carry signals, and complex outcomes happen when many areas coordinate at once.
Activity 5

An Analogy

    The brain is like a huge city. Different neighborhoods have specialized jobs, roads carry messages between them, and the whole city works because many connected systems coordinate at the same time. When the city learns a new routine, it can improve routes or change traffic patterns.

Activity 6

A Story

    Imagine a person learning to ride a bike. At first, many parts of the brain must coordinate vision, balance, movement, memory, and decision-making. The process feels effortful because the networks are still adjusting. With practice, the connections involved become more efficient, so riding begins to feel automatic. In the city model, the brain has improved its routes for that routine.

Activity 7

Key Terms

  • **brain** — The organ that uses networks of specialized cells to process information, regulate the body, generate behavior, and support conscious experience.
  • **neuron** — A specialized cell that communicates through electrical impulses and chemical signals.
  • **glial cell** — A specialized cell that is part of the brain’s working networks alongside neurons.
  • **synapse** — A communication point where neurons use chemical signals.
  • **brain network** — A connected set of brain cells or regions that work together to support functions such as perception, movement, memory, emotion, and decision-making.
  • **specialized brain region** — A brain area that is more involved in certain functions than others.
  • **distributed activity** — Activity spread across many interconnected brain areas rather than happening in only one location.
  • **plasticity** — The brain’s ability to change its connections with learning, experience, development, and injury.
